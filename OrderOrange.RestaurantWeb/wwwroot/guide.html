    00Before you start

    The basics

    Eight things that make every page below make sense

    01Sign in

    Use your username and password, Google, or a one-time code sent to your email. Tick Keep me signed in on a shop device so the app opens without asking again for a month.

    02Your role decides your home

    Owners and managers land on the dashboard. Waiters land on the tablet POS. Every role sees only the pages it is allowed to open.

    03More than one business

    If your account has several stores, a chooser appears at sign-in and the store name in the top bar switches between them at any time.

    04Fourteen languages

    The globe icon in the top bar switches the whole app: English, Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Hindi, Turkish, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Japanese and Chinese. Numbers follow the language too.

    05The bell

    New orders, bookings, messages and reminders arrive in the bell at the top right, and as push notifications on your phone if you allow them once.

    06Search everything

    Ctrl+K or the search box finds pages, products, customers and orders from anywhere.

    07Connect your own systems

    Everything the app does is also a REST API: menus, orders, tables, reservations, customers, bills and reports, with the same login. Accounting packages, CRMs, kitchen screens and head-office systems connect through it. The full reference with live examples is at partner.orderorange.com/api.html.

    08Any country, any currency

    Nothing in OrderOrange is tied to one country. The portal and the customer app speak fourteen languages and read right to left where they should; each shop picks the symbol its prices wear, sets its own tax rate, its own opening hours and its own delivery areas, and prints receipts in the language its kitchen reads. It is used today, and a café in Dubai, Riyadh, Istanbul, Delhi or London runs the same till with its own money on the screen.

    09Large restaurants and chains

    OrderOrange scales from one counter to a large multi-branch restaurant. Every branch is its own business with its own menu, tables, team and reports, all connected under one owner account: one login, a store switcher in the top bar, and the same customer app listing every branch. Roles keep each manager inside their own branch while the owner sees them all.

    10The till app

    Printing to kitchen and receipt printers is done by a small Windows app on the shop PC. It starts with Windows, updates itself, and shows a bell when a table orders by QR code.

    11Your public page

    Customers find you at orderorange.com/your-name. The store page, table QR menus and online booking all come from what you set up here.

    POS

    Ring up a sale in seconds.     02.3

    Tables

    The dining room as it stands, and the tool that arranges it. Across the top, how much of the house is free and how much is sitting — 44 free, 27 occupied tells a host where to put the next party before they have finished asking. Each salon is a card carrying its name, how many tables it holds and how many people those seat; inside it every table shows its number, its seats and what is happening at it: green for free, warm for occupied, a dot for an order still open. Under a seated table is who is there and for how long — Walk-in when the waiter seated them, QR when the guests scanned the code and ordered from their own phones — and a clock running since they sat, which is how a forgotten table becomes obvious from across the room. Tap any table to open its running bill and add a round.

    Every table can carry its own QR code. Print one code per table from Table QR codes and stick it on the table: the code knows both the shop and the table, so a scan opens your menu for that table alone. The guests order from their own phones straight onto that table’s bill, call a waiter or ask for the bill without anyone coming over, and the board marks the table QR so the floor knows the order came from the guests rather than a waiter.

    The floor is drawn, not typed. Drag a salon by the grip in its corner to move it and pull its edge to resize it; the tables inside travel with it, and a salon made smaller pulls its own tables back in rather than stranding them. Salons cannot be dropped on top of one another, so the plan always matches a room a waiter could walk through. Add salon and Add table build it out, the pencil renames, the bin removes, and every move is saved the moment you let go — there is no Save button to forget.

    • Rooms and tables as on the floor
    • Free · seated · QR order · bill requested
    • Tap a table to open its order
    How to
    1. Read the two counters at the top for free and occupied seats across the whole floor.
    2. Green is free, warm is seated, and a dot means an order is still open on that table.
    3. Walk-in means the waiter seated them; QR means the guests scanned the code and ordered themselves.
    4. Watch the clock under a seated table — it counts from when they sat, so a forgotten table stands out.
    5. Tap a table to open its running bill and add a round.
    6. To rearrange: drag a salon by the grip at its corner, pull its edge to resize, and use Add salon, Add table, the pencil and the bin. Nothing needs saving.
    Good to knowTable QR codes are printed from Table QR codes; each code carries the store and table, so a scan opens the right menu and chat.

    03.2

    Kitchen display

    A screen for the pass instead of a printer. Tickets arrive in two lanes — New and Cooking, oldest first, because the oldest ticket is always the next job — and each one counts up from the moment the food was ordered, its edge turning amber and then red as it runs late against your own preparation time. A cook ticks a line off as it is plated and presses the one big button: Start cooking, then Ready. What makes it a kitchen screen rather than a list of web orders is where the tickets come from: online orders AND every round a waiter rings up at a table, which a printer alone never shows you. Each station can be filtered to its own items using your product printer map, so the bar screen shows drinks and the grill screen shows grills. It rings when something new arrives, keeps the display awake, and goes full screen on a tap.

    • Two lanes: new and cooking
    • Timers turn amber, then red
    • One screen per station
    • Recall a bumped ticket for 30 minutes
    How to
    1. Open Kitchen display from Operations on the screen above the pass, then press the full-screen button; it keeps itself awake.
    2. Press Start cooking when the food goes on and Ready when it is plated — the ticket leaves the board.
    3. Tap a line to strike it through as you finish it; every screen in the kitchen sees the same ticks.
    4. Use the station buttons along the top so a screen shows only its own items, from your product printer map.
    Good to knowBumped the wrong ticket? It sits in the strip along the bottom for half an hour — press it to pull it back. The clock on every ticket is the shop server’s, not the tablet’s, so two screens in one kitchen never disagree about how late an order is.

    Smart search

    The search box on the customer home understands people, not just spelling. One query searches restaurants and dishes together, in any of the fourteen languages: "kabab", "kebab" and "كباب" find the same plate. It forgives typos, words typed on the wrong keyboard, Arabizi digits like "3" for ع, merged or split words, and it suggests results while the guest is still typing.     Good to knowThe ✨ assistant button next to the search uses the same engine to take an order by text or voice, and only ever offers real, approved kitchens.

    03.10

    OrderOrange AI

    One assistant, working both sides of the counter.

    For your guests, the sparkle button on the shop page takes an order from start to finish in ordinary language, by typing or by speaking. “2 pizzas and a cola to Al Mouj” becomes a basket, an address and a checkout in one sentence — quantities, sizes, notes and the address read together rather than asked for one at a time. It answers the questions people actually ask: “do you have pizza”, “how much is the delivery”, “where is my order”, “cancel it”, even “what can I get for five rials”. It replies in whatever language the guest wrote in, across fourteen of them, and it forgives spelling — a misspelled kebab still finds the kebab. It never invents a dish: something it cannot find is asked about, not guessed, and it only ever offers real dishes at today’s prices from kitchens that are open.

    For your staff, the same brain sits inside the POS as the order assistant. Key or dictate a whole ticket — “2 koobideh, 1 cola, table 5” — and it lands on that table, which is far faster than hunting a grid at a rush. It answers questions about the shop at the till, does the arithmetic and the clock maths a cashier ends up doing anyway, and Ctrl+K searches pages, products, customers and orders from anywhere in the portal.

    And it tells you what people wanted. Every question a guest typed is kept and grouped by the address it came from, so you can read, in their words, what was asked for and not found — the dish to put on the menu next month is usually in that list.

    It runs on OrderOrange’s own vocabulary, not an outside AI service. Nothing your guests type leaves the platform, there is no per-message bill, it keeps working when somebody else’s service is down, and the same sentence always produces the same basket — which matters when it is taking real money.

    • Orders by text or voice, start to finish
    • Order tracking and menu questions
    • Same assistant inside the POS
    How to
    1. Keep dish names and prices current: the assistant sells exactly what is on your menu and nothing else.
    2. Name dishes the way guests say them; misspellings are forgiven, but a clear name in the customer’s language always wins.
    3. Set delivery areas and fees in Settings so it can quote them when it takes an address.
    4. On the POS, open the order assistant and type or dictate a ticket such as 2 koobideh, 1 cola, table 5.
    5. Read the assistant report every week or two: what guests asked for and did not get is the shortest route to your next dish.
    Good to knowIt understands quantities, sizes, notes and an address in one sentence, and it never invents a dish — an item it cannot find is asked about, not guessed. It is deterministic rather than generative: the same words always produce the same basket, and nothing a guest types is sent to another company.

    03.14

    Loyalty

    Reward the guests who keep coming back. From then on the points arrive by themselves: every order paid at the till, closed at a table or delivered credits the guest, once, as long as the guest is on the order rather than the walk-in. The four tiles count the members and the tier mix, what the points on their cards would cost you, what has already been given back, and thirty days of earning as a bar chart. Almost there lists the guests one visit from a reward and Gone quiet the regulars who have not been in for a month — each with a WhatsApp message ready to send. Underneath, two panels refresh themselves every ten seconds: Online now — who is on your store page or sitting at a table with an open tab this minute — and Open invoices, every bill not yet paid with the guest’s points beside it, so the till can offer a reward before the money is taken.

    How to

    2. On the POS, pick the guest from the customer book instead of the walk-in - that is how the points know whose they are.
    3. Type a name or phone under Find a guest, then Ring up a visit, Use the reward, or Adjust with a reason.

    03.15

    A guest’s card

    Everything about one regular on a single card. The ring fills towards the next reward and turns the colour of their tier; beside it sit the visits, the lifetime points, what they have already redeemed, the last time they came and — for a guest still climbing — how many points to Silver or Gold.     08.3

    Working offline

    OrderOrange can work offline. For shops where the internet drops or is not there at all, an offline edition of the POS keeps selling, printing kitchen tickets and receipts, and holding the day’s orders on the shop PC; everything is sent to the cloud the moment the connection returns, so reports, the customer app and the other branches catch up automatically. It is switched on per shop: open Support, press New ticket, choose Technical problem and write “Offline” as the subject.

    • Sells and prints without internet
    • Syncs when the connection returns
    • Request it with a support ticket
    How to
    1. Open Support from the everything-menu and press New ticket.
    2. Category Technical problem, subject Offline, and tell us which PC or tablet runs the till.
    3. Open the ticket; the team switches the offline edition on for that shop and writes back in the same ticket.
    Good to knowWhile offline the till keeps its own numbering and cash totals; nothing is lost and nothing is double-counted when it syncs.

    Developer API

    Everything the partner app does, your own code can do too. A REST API over HTTPS with the same login: read and write the menu, orders, tables, reservations, customers, bills and reports of your store, in JSON, with verified examples in curl, JavaScript, C# and Python. It is how a CRM, an accounting package, a kitchen screen or a chain’s head-office system connects to OrderOrange. Free with every partner account.

    • Same login, Bearer token
    • 100+ endpoints, JSON over HTTPS
    • CRM, accounting, ERP, kitchen screens
    How to
    1. Create a team member for the integration and give it a custom role with only the permissions it needs.
    2. Sign that member in with POST api/auth/login from your server and keep the token (12 hours, or 30 days with remember).
    3. Read with GET and write with POST or PUT; the reference at partner.orderorange.com/api.html lists every endpoint with real examples.
    Good to knowAccounting reads the daily invoices, a CRM reads and writes customers, an ERP polls the live board; there are no webhooks yet, so poll politely and use the invoice number as your key.

    Frequently asked

    Short answers to what partners ask first

    Can two businesses share one login?

    Yes. An account can own or work in several stores; the chooser appears at sign-in and the top bar switches at any time.

    Is the POS free? What does a POS system cost?

    OrderOrange is free for restaurants and cafés: the POS, the tablet POS, the QR code menu, the kitchen board, reservations, payroll and every report cost nothing and there is no commission. You only pay for your own hardware, and any Windows PC, tablet or phone will do.

    Does it have a QR code menu?

    Yes, built in and free. Print one code per table from Table QR codes; a scan opens the menu for that table, and the guest orders, calls a waiter or reserves. No separate QR menu generator is needed.

    Is there a loyalty programme?

    Yes. Set points per OMR, what a reward costs and what it is worth, a welcome gift and Silver and Gold tiers. Points are added automatically on every paid order; staff can ring up a visit, hand out a reward or correct a balance, and the page shows who is one visit from a reward and which regulars have gone quiet, with a WhatsApp message ready for each.

    Can I run several branches from one warehouse?

    Yes. Mark one place as the central warehouse and every business you own sees it. Branches pull stock from it, head office pushes deliveries to them, and the quantities move between the branches’ own materials so each shop keeps a true count. Inside one shop you can also map every place stock is kept - store, cold room, kitchen, bar - and move stock between them with a full log.

    Does it track waste?

    Yes. Every write-off is recorded with a reason - spoiled, expired, broken, over-made, staff meal, complaint, training - and taken off the shelf at cost. The Waste page shows what the last 7, 30 or 90 days threw away, what share of your stock that is, and which items and reasons cost the most.

    Can it replace a kitchen display system?

    It IS one. Kitchen display is a full-screen board built for the pass: two lanes, a timer on every ticket that turns amber then red as it runs late, tap a line to strike it off, one button to start and one to bump. It shows online orders and rounds sent from the tables in the same place, and each station can be filtered to its own items. Kitchen printers still work alongside it through the till app.

    Is there online ordering for delivery and pickup?

    Yes. Every store gets its own page at orderorange.com with online ordering for delivery or pickup, in 14 languages, and your own couriers can be assigned from the Deliveries page.

    Is there an API?

    Yes, and it is free. The same login gives a Bearer token for a REST API that covers menu, orders, tables, reservations, customers, bills and reports, so a CRM, an accounting package or your own app can connect. The reference with live examples is at partner.orderorange.com/api.html.

    Does it work offline?

    Yes. An offline edition of the POS keeps selling and printing without internet and syncs when the connection is back. Ask for it through Support → New ticket with the subject Offline.

    Can I use OrderOrange in my country?

    Yes. The system is not built around one country: choose your currency symbol in Settings, set your own tax percentage, opening hours and delivery areas, and run the whole portal in any of fourteen languages. Prices are stored exactly as you type them — nothing is converted — so a shop in the Emirates, Saudi Arabia, India, Turkey or Europe works the same way as any other.

    Can I change the currency symbol?

    Yes, in Settings → Currency symbol. Pick one from the list or type your own, and every screen and printout follows at once: the POS, invoices, reports, exports, the kitchen screen and the receipt.

    Does the AI send my customers’ messages to another company?

    No. The assistant understands orders using OrderOrange’s own vocabulary, built into the app, so what a guest types stays on the platform. There is no per-message cost, nothing to sign up for, and it keeps working when an outside service is having a bad day.

    Can the assistant take a whole order by itself?

    Yes, start to finish: it finds the dish, asks the quantity, takes the address, offers the payment methods you accept and places the order, by text or by voice, in fourteen languages. Your staff get the same assistant inside the POS for keying tickets at a rush.

    Which browser and devices are supported?

    Any current Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox on phone, tablet or PC. The app installs as a web app from the browser menu.

    Why did nothing print?

    Printing is done by the till app on the shop PC. Check that it is running in the tray, signed in to the right store, and that the product printers map points at a printer that exists.

    How do customers order from the table?

    Print the table QR codes. A scan opens the menu for that table; the order lands on Live orders and the table turns occupied.

    Can I hide a product from the website but keep it at the counter?

    Yes, tick In-store only on the product. It stays on the POS and the table QR menu.

    Where do reviews come from?

    Customers can review an order after it is delivered; reviews appear under Reports and on your public page.

    What happens to a rejected order?

    The customer is told immediately with the reason you typed; nothing is charged for online card payments.

    How do I change the language?

    The globe icon in the top bar switches the whole app; each team member picks their own.

    Can a waiter see revenue?

    Only if their role allows Reports. Presets keep money pages for owner and manager.

    Where is my data?

    On OrderOrange servers in the cloud, backed up daily. Export any report to Excel whenever you like.
